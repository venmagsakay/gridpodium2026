About Formula 1

Founded in 1950, Formula 1 represents the highest tier of international motorsport. The Grand Prix series takes place across world-renowned tracks in Europe, the Americas, Asia, Australia, and the Middle East.

Each season, constructor teams build bespoke open-wheel race cars to compete for two annual World Championships: one for Drivers and one for Constructors. Teams combine cutting-edge aerodynamics, mechanical engineering, and driving talent to achieve maximum speed and efficiency.

F1 Fundamentals

Advanced Aerodynamics

F1 cars utilize complex front wings, rear wings, and ground-effect floors to maximize downforce and cornering speeds.

Hybrid Power Units

The 2026 Formula 1 hybrid regulations change the power unit to a roughly 50:50 power split between the internal combustion engine (ICE) and the electrical system.

Pit Stop Strategy

Teams change four tires in under 2 seconds during mandatory pit stops, turning race strategy into a high-stakes team effort.
